# Overlap — Marketing Plan

**Constraint:** one person, near-zero budget, building while job-hunting.
**Implication:** every tactic here must be either free or a byproduct of work you're doing anyway. No paid acquisition until after the retention gate — spending to acquire users for a product that doesn't retain is the most common way small teams burn their runway.

---

## 1. The strategic bet

Overlap's growth does not come from marketing. It comes from **the invite link**, which is a product surface, not a campaign. Every plan someone creates puts a branded page in front of five to ten people who did not ask for it, in a context where they are motivated to engage.

Marketing's job is therefore narrow: **get the first hundred groups to exist**, and make the invite artifact good enough that it does the rest.

This means the single highest-leverage marketing decision you will make is how much design time you give the public invite page. More than any post you write.

---

## 2. Positioning

**One-liner:**
> Overlap shows you when your friends are actually free.

**The longer version:**
> Partiful is for the party. Overlap is for the six weeks before it, when nobody can figure out a date.

**The emotional thesis — this is the thing that spreads:**
> Adult friendship isn't failing because people stopped caring. It's failing on logistics.

That sentence is the actual product. It is true, under-discussed, and immediately recognisable to anyone between 25 and 40. Lead with the *problem* everywhere. The app is the punchline, not the pitch.

**Never say:** "social network," "platform," "community," "connect with friends." All four are dead language and all four position you against companies you cannot fight.

---

## 3. Phase 1 — Concierge (weeks 8–16, alongside the pilot)

**Goal: 5 groups. Not 50. Five.**

There is no marketing in this phase. There is recruiting.

- Personally onboard each group. Sit with the anchor person while they create it.
- Weekly 15-minute calls with each anchor.
- **Ask about last week, never about hypotheticals.** "Did you open it Sunday?" not "Would you use this?" People are generous and will lie to be kind.
- Keep a running list of every moment someone was confused. That list is your roadmap.

**What you are looking for:** someone using it without you having reminded them. Until that happens, nothing else matters.

---

## 4. Phase 2 — Build in public (weeks 4–24, starts during the build)

This is your strongest unfair advantage and it costs you almost nothing, because you are doing the work anyway.

You are a designer building a product about friendship logistics while navigating a career transition. That is a genuinely interesting story, and it is *the same audience as your target market* — people in their late twenties and thirties who feel their friendships thinning.

**Cadence: two posts a week. Non-negotiable, low effort, no perfectionism.**

| Format | Where | What |
|---|---|---|
| Design process | Instagram, Twitter/X | Heatmap iterations, invite page variants, the rejected versions |
| The hard problem | Twitter/X, LinkedIn | "How do you show 'free' without lying?" — the A2 audit finding is a genuinely good post |
| Short video | TikTok, Reels | 20–40s. The *problem*, not the app. "The group chat where everyone says 'we should hang' and nobody picks a date." |
| Long-form | Substack or a blog, monthly | The friendship-logistics thesis. This is the piece that gets shared |

**The three posts most likely to travel — write these first:**

1. **"Free doesn't mean available."** Why an empty calendar tells you nothing, and why every group-scheduling tool gets this wrong. Contains a real insight, useful even if nobody downloads anything.
2. **"I audited my own product doc and found eleven ways it would fail."** Build-in-public content is mostly wins. Public self-critique is rare and it earns trust.
3. **"Adult friendship is a scheduling problem."** The thesis piece. This is the one that recruits your audience.

**Rules:** never post a feature announcement without a problem attached. Never post metrics you'd be embarrassed to post if they were bad. Reply to everyone in the first six months.

---

## 5. Phase 3 — Referral by artifact (months 4–8)

Growth becomes a product property.

- **Invite pages carry a soft footer** — "made with Overlap" — that links to a page explaining the problem, not a signup form.
- **The share card** (the cold-start artifact from A10) is designed to be screenshot and dropped into a group chat. Make it beautiful as an image, since most people will never click through.
- **Post-plan Recap cards** are shareable. A photo grid with "6 of you made it happen" is the most natural share moment in the entire product.
- **Target:** each active group produces 0.4 new groups per quarter. Below that, referral isn't working and you need to look at the invite page rather than posting more.

**Ask for the referral at the moment of success**, never on a schedule. Right after a plan is confirmed as happened is the only moment someone feels warmly toward the app.

---

## 6. Phase 4 — Seeded communities (months 5–10)

Where friend groups already congregate and already feel this pain:

- **Run clubs, climbing gyms, board game groups, book clubs.** Pre-existing groups with scheduling pain and a natural organiser. Approach the organiser, not the group.
- **Grad school and alumni cohorts**, 2–5 years out — the exact moment a friend group scatters across a city. Highest-intent segment you will find.
- **Newcomer groups** in Toronto. People rebuilding a social circle from scratch are unusually motivated to adopt tools.
- **Subreddits:** r/toronto, r/AskTO, r/socialskills, r/internetparents. Participate honestly for weeks before ever mentioning the product. Reddit punishes marketing and rewards usefulness.

**Do not do:** Product Hunt before you have retention. A launch spike into a leaky product wastes your one launch.

---

## 7. Phase 5 — Supply side (months 6–12)

Doubles as a revenue motion and an acquisition channel — but note the audit warning: local sales is a headcount business, and one person can realistically service a handful of venues in one neighbourhood.

- Pick **one neighbourhood**, not a city. Ossington, Queen West, Dundas West.
- Approach 20 venues with a concrete offer: *"Groups of 6–8 telling me they're free on your slow nights. Pay only when they show."*
- Start with the ones who need Tuesdays filled, not the ones with a wait list.
- Every partner venue is also a distribution point — table cards, a mention in their newsletter.

---

## 8. Seasonality — plan launches around these

| Window | Why | Push |
|---|---|---|
| **Early January** | Reconnection intent peaks. "We should see each other more" is a resolution | Biggest content push of the year. The thesis essay lands here |
| **Late April / May** | Patio and summer planning starts | Feature push, invite design refresh |
| **September** | Post-summer regrouping, back-to-routine | Second-strongest window |
| **Late November** | Gifting and reunions | Someday List + group gifting launch, if built |

**Avoid launching in July or late December.** Everyone is away, and a launch into a low-availability month produces data that will mislead you.

---

## 9. Metrics that matter

Vanity metrics to ignore: followers, impressions, signups, app downloads.

| Metric | Target by month 8 | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Active groups | 100 | ≥3 members signalled in trailing 14 days |
| Organic group share | ≥ 40% | Groups whose founder arrived via an invite link |
| Invite → signup conversion | ≥ 15% | Is the public page doing its job |
| Groups per referring group per quarter | 0.4 | Is referral compounding |
| CAC | $0 | If this stops being zero before the retention gate, stop |

---

## 10. What not to do

- **No paid ads before M5.** Buying users for a product that doesn't retain is how small teams die.
- **No influencer partnerships.** Friend groups are recruited by their own members, not by strangers.
- **No Product Hunt launch until retention is proven.** You get one, and it should land on a product that holds.
- **No press outreach in year one.** Coverage without retention is a spike and a hangover.
- **No feature marketing.** Nobody wants a heatmap. They want to see their friends.

---

## 11. The first four weeks of marketing, concretely

Do this in parallel with the build, roughly two hours a week.

**Week 1**
- [ ] Reserve handles: Instagram, TikTok, X
- [ ] Write the one-liner and the thesis paragraph. Sit with them, they'll be everywhere
- [ ] Start a private list of every friend group you could plausibly ask

**Week 2**
- [ ] First build-in-public post: the problem, not the product
- [ ] Draft "Adult friendship is a scheduling problem"

**Week 3**
- [ ] Post the heatmap design process — iterations and rejects, not the polished final
- [ ] Approach the first two pilot anchors

**Week 4**
- [ ] Publish the thesis essay
- [ ] Post "Free doesn't mean available"
- [ ] Lock the remaining three pilot groups

By week 8 you should have a small audience that already understands the problem, and five groups ready the day v1 works. That is the entire goal of pre-launch marketing — not demand, just readiness.
